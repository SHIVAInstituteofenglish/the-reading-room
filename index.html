The subject library

Study by subject

Use these shelves to organise your preparation. Each shelf identifies a major area for systematic revision; the descriptions are a guide to topics, not a claim that separate question banks are already included.

SHELF 01

British Literary History

Renaissance, Elizabethan and Jacobean writing, Puritan age, Restoration, Augustan, Romantic, Victorian and Modern periods.

SHELF 02

Literary Criticism & Theory

Classical criticism, Renaissance criticism, Romantic and Victorian critics, New Criticism, Formalism, Structuralism, Deconstruction and Postcolonial criticism.

SHELF 03

Poetry & Prosody

Poets, poems, forms, metres, rhythm, figures of speech, sonnets, elegies, odes and key poetic concepts.

SHELF 04

Drama & Theatre

Shakespeare, revenge tragedy, comedy, Restoration drama, modern drama, playwrights, characters and theatre movements.

SHELF 05

Fiction & Prose

Novelists, novels, characters, narrative forms, essays, biographies, prose works and publication details.

SHELF 06

Indian & World Literature

Indian writing in English, diaspora, African, Caribbean, Canadian and other world-literature references.

SHELF 07

Linguistics & Language

Language concepts and terminology, branches of linguistics, language acquisition and major linguistic distinctions.

SHELF 08

Chronology & Matching

Arrange authors, works, literary periods and movements; practise match-the-following and sequence-based revision.

SHELF 09

Previous-Year Practice

Attempt year-labelled questions under timed conditions, review the key and maintain an error log.

A practical routine

How to use the Reading Room

Build a repeatable cycle rather than reading topics without testing recall.

01 — Read and map

Choose one shelf. Make a compact timeline of authors, works, dates, movements and key terms.

02 — Retrieve from memory

Close your notes and recall names, titles, concepts and chronological relationships before checking.

03 — Practise questions

Attempt relevant previous-year questions without looking at the answer key. Note why each distractor is incorrect.

04 — Review errors

Maintain an error log and revisit weak topics at spaced intervals. Cross-check facts with reliable references.
