Study by Subject

Use these shelves to organise your preparation. Each shelf identifies a major area for systematic revision.

SHELF 01

British Literary History

Renaissance, Elizabethan and Jacobean writing, Puritan age, Restoration, Augustan, Romantic, Victorian and Modern periods.

SHELF 02

Literary Criticism & Theory

Classical criticism, Romantic critics, New Criticism, Formalism, Structuralism, Deconstruction and Postcolonial criticism.

SHELF 03

Poetry & Prosody

Poets, poems, forms, metres, rhythm, figures of speech, sonnets, elegies, odes and poetic concepts.

SHELF 04

Drama & Theatre

Shakespeare, revenge tragedy, comedy, Restoration drama, modern drama, playwrights and theatre movements.

SHELF 05

Fiction & Prose

Novelists, novels, characters, narrative forms, essays, biographies and prose works.

SHELF 06

Indian & World Literature

Indian writing in English, diaspora, African, Caribbean, Canadian and other world-literature references.

SHELF 07

Linguistics & Language

Language concepts, branches of linguistics, language acquisition and major linguistic distinctions.

SHELF 08

Chronology & Matching

Arrange authors, works, literary periods and movements; practise matching and sequence-based revision.

SHELF 09

Previous-Year Practice

Attempt year-labelled questions under timed conditions, review the key and maintain an error log.

A practical routine

How to Use the Reading Room

Build a repeatable cycle rather than reading topics without testing recall.

01 — Read and Map

Choose one shelf. Make a compact timeline of authors, works, dates, movements and key terms.

02 — Retrieve from Memory

Close your notes and recall names, titles, concepts and chronological relationships before checking.

03 — Practise Questions

Attempt online tests and previous-year questions without looking at the answer key.

04 — Review Errors

Maintain an error log and revisit weak topics at spaced intervals. Cross-check facts with reliable references.
